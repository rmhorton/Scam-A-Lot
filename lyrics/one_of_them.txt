ONE OF THEM

Brisk country-folk patter song with warm, straight-faced lead and small community chorus. Acoustic guitar, banjo, fiddle, bass, and light drums. Vocals begin on the first beat and continue without instrumental breaks. Bridge softens; final verse turns regretful. No instrumental intro or coda; end on the final sung line. Target 3:30.

[VERSE 1]

We always taught our children
To study and succeed;
We sent them off to college
To learn the things they’d need.

We taught them to think for themselves,
To question and explore—
But not to tear down everything
That we had done before.

[CHORUS]

We helped them pay for college;
They treat us like an ATM.
We sent them out to make us proud;
They came back one of them.

They still bear our family names
And legally they’re kin—
But anyone who isn’t us
Must be one of them.

[VERSE 2]

Minneapolis burned down;
Seattle's riots rage each day.
We watched it all on cable
From a thousand miles away.

Our children say their neighborhoods
Are peaceful and they’re fine—
But they have only lived there;
We watch the news online.

[VERSE 3]

Alice earned a master’s
In medieval Spanish prose;
It cost a hundred thousand bucks—
That’s how much she owes.

She rents one room, sells coffee,
Feeds strangers when she can,
And dates an Argentinian—
We don’t know where that began.

[FAST PATTER]

Brenda came home pierced and tattooed,
With Katie at her side.
Her mother set another place;
Her father nearly died.

Eric studied circuits
For eight expensive years;
Then told us, “Call an electrician.”
So much for engineers!

Sam has a job in finance;
He makes money at a crazy pace.
He paid ten thousand dollars
For a bicycle to race.
Now he rides around in spandex
Like a clown from outer space!

Bobby studied DNA,
And calls Noah’s Ark a myth.
We sent him out to learn the truth—
And he came back with this?

[SHORT CHORUS]

Where did they learn this craziness?
They do the things that we condemn!
We sent them out to make us proud;
They came back one of them.

[VERSE 4]

That vegan is our nephew;
That immigrant’s his friend.
The trans coach at the high school
Was our football star back then.

The liberal in the city
Was the smart kid that we praised—
Our enemies look strangely like
The children that we raised.

[BRIDGE - QUIETER]

Out here dogs can wander,
And woodsmoke smells like home;
But rules are really different
When you don't live alone.

When thousands share the sidewalks,
Subways, parks, and air,
You have to bag your dog poop
And can’t send smoke everywhere.

A private choice turns public
When you live in such a crowd.
Maybe it’s not really “wokeness”
To say these things are not allowed.

[FINAL VERSE]

The town is safe and quiet now;
Some children never strayed.
We called the others “one of them”
Until they stayed away.

The table isn’t empty,
But holidays have changed.
We fought to save the family—
And left our own estranged.

We keep their bedrooms as they were,
Though they do these things we can't condone.
We kept the town we thought we wanted—
But we wish they could come home.
