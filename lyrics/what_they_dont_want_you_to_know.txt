What they don't want you to know

1950s American cabaret comedy. Brisk piano-led swing, upright bass, brushed drums. Intelligent humorous lecture-song with rapid-fire lyrics, conversational singing, theatrical phrasing, sophisticated wordplay, crisp enunciation, continuous vocals, short musical fills, polished live stage performance. 
Section 1: Bright, optimistic swing.
Section 2: Music softens. More restrained, reflective, and wistful, with lighter accompaniment and a more conversational vocal.
Section 3: Tempo and intensity build rapidly. Increasingly animated and breathless, culminating in a triumphant finale.

[SECTION 1 – Wonder]
[Optimistic: Bright, curious, joyful. Wonder and excitement about discovery.]

Wouldn't it be great... 
To discover something really new?
To do something that's never been done, 
Or learn something no one knew?

To solve the ancient mysteries
That have puzzled us for centuries.

To build a better battery.
Or cheaper fusion energy.

To cure Alzheimer's disease.
Or put an end to allergies.

To prove a famous theorem.
Or prevent tooth decay.
To find another planet.
Or discover DNA.

To decypher ancient languages
Or predict the weather right.
To understand the universe 
Or bring a new idea to light.

To build a bridge that's stronger. 
Or write better code.
To stop a deadly virus. 
Or make a safer road.

To make our water cleaner 
Or make the deserts bloom.
To build a faster rocketship 
Or cities on the Moon.

Every great discovery begins when someone questions yesterday.
Somebody looks around and dares to say...
"What if we could see things in a new and different way?"

To make an observation that the world will celebrate.
To solve our biggest problems before it is too late.
Oh! Wouldn't it be great?


[SECTION 2 — Reality]
[Sobering: More reflective and earnest. The dream meets reality. Calm, thoughtful, slightly melancholy.]

Great ideas need financing.
New research isn't free.
Before you prove your theory,
You have to raise a lot of money.

A better way of doing things
Can put the old guard out of business.
Suddenly they're not so keen 
About survival of the fittest

Some leaders love discoveries.
Some wish they'd go away.
Truth can be inconvenient
To those in power today.

They'll ask for better evidence.
They'll ask, "How do you know?"
They'll repeat your experiments
To see if they still hold.

They'll search your work for weak spots
And try to prove it wrong.
If your idea survives all this,
It probably belongs.

[SECTION 3 — The Shortcut]
[Frenetic: Tempo and energy increase. Excited, persuasive, increasingly breathless.]

But here's what they don't tell you;
There's a much less painful route.
You don't need years of testing...
I have figured it all out!

[1. Opposition proves you're right]
If they oppose your project,
That proves you're on the right track!
They're terrified you'll prove them wrong—
That's why they hold you back!

[2. Experts are disqualified by expertise]
Experts spend their lives in school
Getting titles and degrees.
Experts serve the system;
They safeguard its beliefs.

The system's a cathedral
And the experts are its priests.
Experts are disqualified
By their own expertise!

[3. Consensus proves corruption]
Experts train new experts
To think the way they do.
That's how they reach consensus...
And never agree with you.

[4. Common sense beats specialized knowledge]
You don't need fancy textbooks!
You don't need PhDs!
Common sense will tell you more
Than all their pedigrees!

[5. Suppression proves importance]
They'll say you spread 'misinformation'
That your ideas just aren't so.
That's how you know you've stumbled on
What they don't want you to know.

The more they try to stop you
The stronger grows your claim.
Opposition is all the proof you need—
They're all in on the game!


[FINAL CHORUS]
[Triumphant certainty. Big finish. Everyone joins in.]

The more they try to stop you,
The more convinced you'll grow!
Resistance only proves you've found
What they don't want you to know!

Resistance only proves you've found
What they don't want you to know!
