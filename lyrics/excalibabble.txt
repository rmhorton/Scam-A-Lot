# EXCALIBABBLE

Mock-medieval English court-pageant music staged as a theatrical ensemble song. Moderate, steady processional pulse; earnest, straight-faced performance. Modal melodies, open-fifth drones, heraldic trumpet fanfares, lute, cittern, recorders, viols, tabor drum, and occasional crumhorn. A strong male Herald carries the introduction and narrative verses; the ensemble answers with bold communal choruses. Give “Excalibur” and “Excalibabble” closely related, instantly memorable melodic hooks. In the labeled bridge, thin the accompaniment to tabor and drone while individual crowd voices deliver the contradictory claims in clear alternating solo lines; allow natural overlap over sustained final notes. At [ALL], restore the full ensemble and instrumentation. The male Herald then resumes the narrative and delivers the prophecy ceremonially. Measured, intelligible pacing throughout; strong concise final chorus and hard ending.

Exclude: 
contemporary pop duet, romantic duet, modern Broadway pop, pop ballad, Spanish guitar, flamenco guitar, classical guitar, nylon-string guitar, modern acoustic guitar, electric guitar, electric bass, synthesizers, drum kit, Latin rhythms, Mediterranean folk, bluegrass mandolin, audience laughter, chuckling, applause, audience reactions, live-recording ambience, extended instrumental breaks, long intro, long outro, added lyric repetitions
